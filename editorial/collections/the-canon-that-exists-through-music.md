# The Canon That Exists Through Music · قصائد خلدها الغناء

## The professor’s perspective

I’d start with the upside-down coffee cup in Nizar Qabbani’s _The Fortune Teller_. It’s a scene you could come across at a family table. In Abdel Halim Hafez’s voice, the fortune teller’s warning becomes something an audience can wait for together. What does a melody make people remember about a poem, and what do they discover when they return to the page?

## What to teach in each poem

### 1 · الأطلال

**Professor’s note.** Nagi gives a lost love the ruins of an old Arabic ode. Umm Kulthum’s _Al-Atlal_ draws on this poem and his _Al-Wadaʿ_, and her performance made the lover’s plea to be set free one of the lines audiences remembered. The plea is worth returning to after the opening address to the heart. [Performance text](https://www.ummkulthum.com/ar/songs/al-atlal/)

### 2 · رباعيات الخيام

**Professor’s note.** In Ahmed Rami’s Arabic rendering, a voice calls before dawn and tells the sleeper to fill a cup while there’s still time. Umm Kulthum’s _Rubaiyat al-Khayyam_ gave listeners Arabic lines they could carry into a conversation about yesterday, tomorrow, or a day they had wasted. [Arabic edition](https://torath.gov.eg/book/Book_bookAuthority_rbAEyAt-AlxyAm_2014) · [Performance text](https://ummkulthum.com/en/songs/rubaiyat-el-khayyam/)

### 3 · نهج البردة

**Professor’s note.** Shawqi opens with a gazelle and a wounded lover, then turns toward praise of the Prophet Muhammad. His ode answers al-Busiri’s _Burda_, and Umm Kulthum’s _Nahj al-Burda_ brought that conversation between devotional poems into the home through song. The gazelle is a surprising place for a first-time reader to meet that tradition. [Shawqi’s collected poems](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/40/) · [Performance text](https://www.ummkulthum.com/en/songs/nahj-al-burda/)

### 4 · أراك عصي الدمع

**Professor’s note.** Someone admires the speaker’s composure. His reply lets us in on what he feels after dark. Umm Kulthum sang _Araka ʿAsiyy al-Damʿ_ in several settings over her career, giving a tenth-century line about withholding tears a familiar place in modern Arabic song. [Performance text](https://www.ummkulthum.com/en/songs/araka-asiyy-al-dam/)

### 5 · سلوا قلبي

**Professor’s note.** Shawqi starts by asking others to question his heart and arrives at a claim about what it takes to get what you want. Umm Kulthum’s _Salou Qalbi_ helped the line about wishes and resolve travel on its own. You can imagine someone quoting it to a friend who is waiting for their luck to change. [Shawqi’s collected poems](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/6/) · [Performance text](https://www.ummkulthum.com/ar/songs/salou-qalbi/)

### 6 · قارئة الفنجان

**Professor’s note.** Qabbani puts the fear in the fortune teller’s eyes before she has said a word. Abdel Halim Hafez’s _Qariʾat al-Finjan_ made an everyday act of reading coffee grounds into a widely remembered scene about the trouble love can bring. Follow the shift from the man describing the cup to the woman who gets to tell him his future. [Arabic poem](https://nizarq.com/ar/poem51.html) · [Recording](https://music.apple.com/us/album/qareat-el-fengan/842534661)

### 7 · رسالة من تحت الماء

**Professor’s note.** The speaker asks the person he loves to rescue him from the love that is drowning him. In Abdel Halim Hafez’s _Risala min Taht al-Maʾ_, that impossible request becomes the line a singer or a listener can return to whenever ordinary words for heartbreak feel too small. [Arabic poem](https://nizarq.com/ar/poem43.html) · [Recording](https://music.apple.com/us/album/resala-men-taht-el-maa-live/843695551)

### 8 · أعطني الناي وغن

**Professor’s note.** Gibran’s _The Procession_ keeps returning to a request for the flute. Fairuz’s _Aʿtini al-Nay_ made that invitation a way into Gibran’s Arabic poetry for listeners who first knew it as a song. Notice how much the poem asks of a single sound when it imagines the flute outlasting us. [Qatar National Library edition](https://ediscovery.qnl.qa/ar/islandora/object/QNL%3A00005551) · [Recording](https://play.anghami.com/song/2856390)

### 9 · جادك الغيث

**Professor’s note.** Ibn al-Khatib wishes for rain to bless a time in his life as if it were a person. Fairuz’s _Gadak El Ghayth_ gives listeners a way into the Andalusi _muwashshah_, a strophic poetic form with a long singing tradition. Its remembered nights in al-Andalus can make a place the listener has never visited feel like somewhere they miss. [Arabic text](https://poetry.dct.gov.ae/poems/72139-جادك-الغيث-إذا-الغيث-همى) · [Fairuz’s recording](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1420452905)

### 10 · إلى أمي

**Professor’s note.** Darwish begins with bread, coffee, and touch. Marcel Khalife’s _My Mother_ carried those details into a shared language of homesickness. The song is close enough to sing about your own mother, and the poem’s wish to return lets that longing reach toward Palestine. [Smithsonian Folkways recording](https://folkways.si.edu/marcel-khalife/promises-of-the-storm)

### 11 · ريتا والبندقية

**Professor’s note.** Darwish puts a rifle between the speaker’s eyes and Rita. Marcel Khalife’s _Rita and the Rifle_ gave listeners a song in which a lover’s name and the fact of war keep being heard together. The remembered tenderness matters because the rifle is already there in the first lines. [Smithsonian Folkways recording and liner notes](https://folkways.si.edu/marcel-khalife/promises-of-the-storm)

### 12 · إني خيرتك فاختاري

**Professor’s note.** Qabbani makes the speaker insist that his lover choose. Kazem al-Saher’s _Ikhtari_ made that demand a line concert audiences could sing back. On the page, the promise to accept her decision sounds different once you hear how narrowly he describes her options. [Arabic poem](https://nizarq.com/ar/poem2.html) · [Recording](https://play.anghami.com/song/104372)

### 13 · زيديني عشقاً

**Professor’s note.** The speaker asks for more love in the same breath that makes desire sound like a fever. Kazem al-Saher’s _Zidini ʿIshqan_ gave Qabbani’s repeated demand a refrain audiences could take up themselves. Read it beside _Ikhtari_ and hear how an imperative changes when the speaker is the one asking for more. [Official music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ACXtwSqQOxw)
