The flow that ships today asks six questions about poetry before showing any. Both directions below invert that. Tap through each phone — they are live.

What the current flow gets wrong

Not the craft — the hex comb, the bilingual weighting, the honest two doors are all better than most apps ship. The problem is upstream of the pixels: the flow spends its entire attention budget on self-report.

Six screens, no poem
The product's whole proposition is a line, set well — and the flow withholds it until after the sixth tap. Calm, Duolingo and Spotify all deliver a taste of the core emotion inside the first screen; this delivers a taxonomy quiz.
Declared ≠ revealed
"Which images stay with you?" asks a reader to already know their own taxonomy. People answer aspirationally. Meanwhile preferenceWeighting.js only needs labels — and a poem someone actually kept carries them more honestly than a checkbox.
Step 5 is a status test
"Easy reading, or a challenge?" makes a reader rank their own Arabic in public. It is the highest-abandonment shape a question can take, and the answer is inferable for free from reading posture plus whether they open a translation.
Locked doors
Both CTAs sit dimmed and inert until posture is picked, with no copy explaining the lock — friction placed at the exact moment the cost of leaving is lowest.
No payoff
The flow ends on navigate('/'). Six answers, and the reward is the identical screen a skipper gets. Nothing is shown to have changed, so nothing was worth answering.
Write-once, invisible
Answers never resurface. No "you're seeing this because you said grief", no one-tap correction — so a wrong answer is a permanent, unattributable tax on the feed.

The mocks

Both keep the existing scoring engine untouched — answers stay a weight, never a filter — and both end on the same reveal. They differ on where the signal comes from.

Direction A · recommended

The First Draw

Zero abstract questions. Three real poems, chosen to sit far apart on mood, century and density, so three taps triangulate the space. Onboarding and reading are the same act; a reader who quits after one tap has still read a poem. ~25 seconds, all of it spent on poetry.

Three poems. Tell us which ones stay.

Direction B · the minimal cut

One Sheet, One Door

Keeps declared preference but collapses six gated screens into one sheet answerable in any order, nothing required, no locks, no back button to regret. A poem sits at the top the whole time and re-draws as you tap, so every answer has a visible consequence before you commit.

What actually changes

Beyond the screens — the dimensions a flow like this usually forgets.
