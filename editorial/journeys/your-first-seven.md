# Your First Seven · سبع سماوات

Keep the mock’s seven heavens. The sequence starts at an abandoned campsite and ends with a living poet asking strangers to return a lost country.

| Heaven                 | Poem and reading cue                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 · The abandoned camp | **Imruʾ al-Qays, opening of the Muʿallaqa · قفا نبك.** Hear the speaker ask two companions to stop and weep. Introduce the classical ode through the traces of a departed beloved’s camp. Offer a labeled excerpt and a way to read the full ode. [Ithra edition](https://www.ithra.com/files/6516/0984/6883/Al_Muallaqat.pdf)                                   |
| 2 · Grief              | **Al-Khansa, “Lament for a Brother,” for Sakhr.** Move from a place that carries an absence to a voice trying to keep a person present. Ask when her attention turns from weeping to what she wants others to remember about him. Introduce elegy, or _rithāʾ_. [Bilingual anthology](https://penguinrandomhousesecondaryeducation.com/book/?isbn=9780375712432) |
| 3 · Pride              | **Al-Mutanabbi, “Wā Ḥarra Qalbāh” · واحرّ قلباه.** Begin at the reproach to his patron and hear the famous boast bring horses, night, weapons, paper, and pen into one breath. Ask what the poet needs his audience to hear. [Arabic text](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem20938.html)                                                                               |
| 4 · Desire             | **Wallada bint al-Mustakfi, “Must separation mean we have no way to meet?”** Hear her speak her own longing. Offer Ibn Zaydun’s _Poem in N_ as a door into the Andalusi love lyric. [Bilingual anthology](https://penguinrandomhousesecondaryeducation.com/book/?isbn=9780375712432)                                                                             |
| 5 · Rain               | **Badr Shakir al-Sayyab, “Rain Song” · أنشودة المطر.** Start with Iraq’s hunger and hear the repeated _maṭar_ become a promise of renewal. Put it beside the opening ode and ask how each makes rhythm carry meaning. [SOAS study and translation](https://eprints.soas.ac.uk/33610/1/11010371.pdf)                                                              |
| 6 · Home               | **Mahmoud Darwish, “To My Mother” · إلى أمي.** Start with bread, coffee, and touch. Let those particulars lead into the poem’s Palestinian history and public life. [Study of the poem](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_286997_b5763fcdb3e39ae8915913c2bb1d2b.pdf)                                                                                               |
| 7 · Home, again        | **Dunya Mikhail, “I Was in a Hurry” · كنت مسرعة.** She asks for a lost country back as if someone might find it on the road. Listen for what she asks of the people passing by. [Mikhail’s bilingual reading](https://www.pw.org/content/dunya_mikhail_with_the_national_arab_orchestra)                                                                         |
